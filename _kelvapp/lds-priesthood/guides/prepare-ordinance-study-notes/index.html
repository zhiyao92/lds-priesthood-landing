How to prepare ordinance study notes with the official source in view

A careful personal-study routine for ordinance references: check current guidance, record questions and keep private reflections discreet.

By Kelvapp · 7 November 2026 · 3 min read

Looking up an ordinance reference can raise several questions at once: What does this instruction mean? Where is the current source? Is there something I should ask an authorized leader before proceeding? Those are worth separating instead of trying to solve them by rereading the same paragraph faster.

LDS Priesthood: Ordinances is an independent reference and study aid. It can help you find material and review it at your own pace. It does not grant priesthood authority, permission or authorization, and it does not replace current Church guidance.

Begin with one study question

Choose a specific topic rather than opening every reference at once. You might want to better understand a responsibility, locate an instruction or prepare a question for a leader. Write your question in ordinary language before you start.

Keep “I want to understand this” separate from “I am authorized to perform this.” Reading a reference can support study, but authority and permission must be addressed through the current official guidance and appropriate authorized leaders.

Find the reference, then check the source

The app includes search across ordinances, prayers, offices and topics, plus favorites for material you revisit. Use these tools to locate the relevant subject, then check the current General Handbook on the Church’s official website.

Record the section title or link in your own study notes and the date you checked it. Read enough surrounding material to understand the scope. A familiar phrase or a saved reference should not be treated as proof that every detail is current or applicable to your circumstances.

Sort your notes into three parts

A simple page can keep your study focused:

  • Source: where the current instruction is published.
  • Understanding: what you think the passage means, written in your own words.
  • Question: anything you still need to clarify with an authorized leader.

Keep your interpretation visibly separate from the source. This is especially useful if you return weeks later and no longer remember which sentence was your own summary. A neat note is helpful; a neat note mistaken for an official instruction is less helpful.

Use reading tools for preparation

If you have Pro, guided mode and read-aloud can help you review a sequence, while focus mode offers larger, distraction-reduced text and keeps the screen awake. Try these tools during personal preparation so you understand how they work.

Choose the reading method that helps you pay attention. You do not need every mode, theme or option to have a useful study session. Follow current guidance and authorized leaders regarding actual practice rather than assuming a particular app workflow is appropriate in every setting.

Bring a specific question to a leader

If something remains unclear, include the section you read and explain the point you are unsure about. “I read this section and want to check how it applies here” gives the conversation a clearer starting point than a general request to explain everything.

Do not use an app’s checklist, wording or completion indicator as evidence of permission. When official guidance or an authorized leader’s direction differs from your understanding, resolve that uncertainty before treating your notes as a plan.

Keep reflections personal and discreet

The app’s optional Pro journal can hold personal impressions. Focus on your own learning and feelings. Avoid recording sensitive details about other people, health concerns or private conversations simply because there is a convenient place to type them.

Review the app’s privacy information before writing anything sensitive. Its published description includes device storage and syncing, so do not assume that a journal entry is an offline-only record.

A good study session can end with a better question rather than a finished checklist. Explore LDS Priesthood as a convenient reference, keep the official source close, and let careful preparation include asking when you are unsure.
